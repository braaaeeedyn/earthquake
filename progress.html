Geomagnetic precursor · real-data evaluation

Reading the field for the shake before it comes.

A fused CNN + GNN + Transformer model learns from station magnetometer traces to estimate the chance of a nearby earthquake within seven days. Tested on ~14 years of real INTERMAGNET + USGS data across two regions — California and Japan — with identical methodology. This console reports whether it found any signal.

Two-region precursor test

Real data, both regions, identical methodology: a fixed grid of proximity radius × magnitude cutoff, evaluated on leakage-safe chronological test splits, 5 seeds per cell. Nothing is tuned to flatter any metric. ROC-AUC measures discrimination (no skill = 0.50); PR-AUC is shown over the positive base rate (no skill = 1.0×).

What's next

    Honest disclaimer. Every number on this page comes from real INTERMAGNET + USGS data over ~14 years, on leakage-safe chronological test splits with nothing tuned to flatter the result. The finding is essentially chance-level discrimination — no reliable 7-day precursor signal — which is consistent with geomagnetic earthquake prediction being scientifically contested. This is a research prototype, not an alerting system.
