Estimated shaking intensity (MMI): answers to your questions

SeismicSoCal · 23 October 2026 · Braedyn Thompson

Data validation

  • Detect model: trained on labelled 30 s single-station windows, not continuous data: 34,377 with a P arrival (8,238 quakes, M 2.0–7.2) and 16,366 without (random quiet times, plus the old live system’s false alarms), half and half in each batch. Mixed, continuous data is tested separately, by replaying 80 held-out days through the live system.
    • Test (7,303 windows): AUC 0.9998, MCC 0.886 (STA/LTA: AUC 0.816). MCC (Matthews correlation coefficient) = (TP·TN − FP·FN) / √((TP+FP)(TP+FN)(TN+FP)(TN+FN)), from −1 (all wrong) via 0 (chance) to +1 (all right). Here 4,562 quake windows flagged (TP), 402 missed (FN, 8 %), 0 quiet windows flagged (FP), 2,339 passed (TN). Always answering “quake” is 68 % accurate but has MCC 0. AUC: a quake window outscores a quiet one 99.98 % of the time.
  • Size model: trained on all M 3+ quakes in the region from 2000 to Aug 2026 with at least 3 of our stations within 200 km, plus a random 4,000 M 2–3 quakes, using only stations within 100 km (6,243 quakes). For each quake it sees 30 s of shaking at every station that recorded it (each recording’s shape, how big it was, and the station’s distance) and learns to output the catalogue magnitude; in training the location, timing and stations are randomly disturbed to mimic live conditions. Both models are split 70/15/15 by time and by quake; no quake used to train one is in the other’s test set.
  • Ground-motion equation (log10 PGV = a + bM + c log10R + dR + e log10(VS30/631)): fitted on the Size model’s 4,370 training quakes (2000 to July 2019, 25,526 station records). Its 937 test quakes are from 2021–2026.
  • Magnitude vs distance: most records are M 3–4 at 50–200 km. Only 7 training records are within 5 km and only 29 quakes are M 5 or larger, so close, large quakes are outside the calibrated range.
  • DYFI check: 57 quakes of M 4.05–5.22 (2022–2026), all after the training period. The 29 older ones were used to fit the offset; the newer ones only for scoring: 1,868 cells from the 27 of them our stations recorded.
  • 0.42 is the mean absolute error (|estimate − DYFI| averaged over cells), not the signed error. The bias is +0.06 and 93 % of cells are within one level. The reference is USGS “Did You Feel It?” intensity in 10 km cells with at least 3 responses, not ShakeMap or station intensities.

Calibration

  • The +0.43 offset is the median of (DYFI − model) over the 29 older quakes, with each quake’s event correction applied. The 28 test quakes were excluded, and the offset was not changed after scoring.

Implementation

  • Very close to the quake: the formula measures distance along the ground from the epicentre (the point on the surface above the quake) and never lets it drop below 1 km. It ignores depth, although a typical quake starts about 6–7 km down, so right above a quake its prediction keeps climbing even though the quake is still several km below you. We have almost no recordings within 5 km to check this, and at 5–20 km the formula predicts only about half of what our stations recorded. The usual fix is to count the depth in the distance.
  • PGV and its units: PGV (peak ground velocity) is the fastest the ground moved. Each station records three directions (up-down, north-south, east-west), and we take the single largest value of the three, which is almost always a sideways one (up-down is largest in only 7 % of recordings). The formula works in metres per second; the Worden conversion needs centimetres per second, so the value is multiplied by 100.
  • Weak shaking: Worden’s conversion from PGV to intensity is two straight lines, a gentle one for weak shaking (up to 3.4 cm/s) and a steeper one above, and the result is capped between I and X. USGS ShakeMap adds a third line for the very weakest shaking; we tried it and it matched people’s reports worse (error 0.46 vs 0.42), so we left it out.
